I keep my notes in the margins, in pencil, in a hand that even I can barely read by spring. This is a record of one winter's reading — what stayed with me, what fell away, and the several things I was sure of in December and had abandoned by March.

December

Began three books, finished none, and did not mind. There is a season for finishing and a season for grazing, and the short dark days seem made for the second. A paragraph read twice by lamplight is worth a chapter skimmed in daylight.

January

Returned to a novel I had dismissed years ago and found it had been waiting patiently to prove me wrong. The book had not changed. I had. This is the humbling arithmetic of rereading: the text holds still while the reader keeps moving.

A margin is a place to argue with a book without interrupting it.

February

A month of poetry, mostly. I notice I underline less than I used to. Perhaps I trust the memory more, or perhaps I have simply learned that the lines worth keeping tend to keep themselves. The rest is weather.

By the end I had filled the back pages with a list of things to read next, which is the only reliable sign that the winter's reading has done its work: not conclusions, but appetite.
